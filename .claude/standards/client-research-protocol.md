# Client research protocol

What "research every client" means in practice, before any agent drafts
outreach, a proposal, or scoping advice about a specific firm. Go
through all five steps — skipping to the draft is exactly what
`advisory-standard.md` rules out.

## 1. Regulatory profile
- Work out which regulated activity(ies) this firm actually appears to
  carry out — cross-reference against the full list in `CLAUDE.md` §3,
  don't assume it's just credit broking because that's the current base.
- Check the FCA Register for their existing permissions/status
  (Authorised / Registered / Appointed Rep / Not Found).
- Note any gap between what they appear to be doing and what they're
  actually permitted for — that gap is usually the whole opportunity.

## 2. Business profile
From Companies House and their website:
- Size (employee count if visible), how long they've been trading,
  ownership structure (sole trader / partnership / limited company —
  this also determines the PECR treatment per `CLAUDE.md` §7).
- Apparent scale of the regulated activity where you can tell (e.g. how
  many finance deals a dealership looks to be doing) — enough to judge
  whether they're genuinely the small-to-medium target profile
  `CLAUDE.md` §2 describes, or actually too large/small to be a fit.

## 3. Signals of change
Anything suggesting this firm's regulatory or business position is about
to shift: expanding into a new product line, an authorisation nearing
its anniversary, a firm connected to a live theme in the
**📚 FCA & Consumer Credit Research Repository** (e.g. motor finance
redress exposure). Check the repository for anything relevant to this
firm's specific activity before drafting.

## 4. Fit assessment across all three service lines
Don't default to one service line. Explicitly weigh:
- **FCA Launch Pad** — unauthorised or needs a permission variation.
- **FCA Guard** — authorised but under-resourced on ongoing compliance.
- **Health Check & Rescue** — visible signs of an existing problem.

Note which one fits and why, in one line, before writing anything else.

## 5. Only then, draft
The client-facing content should visibly reflect what you found in
steps 1–4 — reference their actual activity and situation, not a
generic line that could apply to any firm. If you couldn't find enough
in steps 1–3 to say something specific, say what's missing rather than
padding the draft with filler.
