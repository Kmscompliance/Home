# 1. Purpose and scope
This policy sets out how [Insert firm legal name] (“we”, “us”, “the firm”) identifies and verifies its customers, understands the purpose of each relationship, assesses customer risk, and keeps customer information up to date. Together, these steps are called **customer due diligence** (CDD).
CDD protects our customers, our firm and the wider financial system. It helps us to prevent money laundering, terrorist financing, sanctions breaches, identity theft and fraud, and it gives us the information we need to treat customers fairly.
This policy is written to meet:
- the MLRs (where they apply to us – see our Anti-Money Laundering Policy), including the amendments made by SI 2026/621, most of which came into force on 30 June 2026;
- SYSC 6.1.1R and, where it applies to us, SYSC 6.3 of the FCA Handbook, and the FCA’s Financial Crime Guide;
- HM Treasury-approved JMLSG guidance for our sector, where relevant;
- UK sanctions law; and
- the UK GDPR and Data Protection Act 2018, which govern how we use and keep the personal data we collect.
It applies to all staff [and appointed representatives – delete if you have none] who onboard customers, handle customer information or process payments. Our regulated activities are: [Specify the regulated activities / permissions your firm holds or is applying for].

# 2. Key terms
- **Customer due diligence (CDD):** identifying the customer and verifying their identity using documents, data or information from a reliable and independent source; identifying and verifying any beneficial owner; and understanding the purpose and intended nature of the relationship or transaction.
- **Simplified due diligence (SDD):** reduced measures that a firm within the MLRs may apply where it has assessed a relationship or transaction as presenting a low risk (regulation 37 of the MLRs).
- **Enhanced due diligence (EDD):** additional measures applied where the risk is higher (regulation 33 of the MLRs), such as further verification, establishing source of funds or wealth, senior management approval and closer monitoring.
- **Beneficial owner:** for a company, any individual who ultimately owns or controls more than 25% of the shares or voting rights, or who otherwise exercises control; for other entities, the individuals who ultimately own or control them.
- **Politically exposed person (PEP):** an individual entrusted with a prominent public function, in the UK or abroad, together with their family members and known close associates.
- **Business relationship:** a business, professional or commercial relationship which is expected, when contact is first made, to have an element of duration.
- **Occasional transaction:** a one-off transaction outside a business relationship.
- **Source of funds:** where the money for a particular transaction comes from. **Source of wealth:** how the customer came to have their overall wealth.

# 4. Standard verification measures
**4.1 Information we collect.** For individuals we collect, at a minimum: full name; date of birth; residential address; and contact details. For business customers we also collect: registered name and number; registered office and principal place of business; the names of directors or partners; and details of beneficial owners.
**4.2 How we verify identity.** We verify using one of the following methods, chosen according to risk:
- **Electronic verification** through [Insert provider name], which checks identity against multiple independent data sources. Where available, we use a provider certified under the UK digital identity and attributes trust framework. We set the matching standard at [Insert standard, e.g. “two independent sources matching name and address, and one matching name and date of birth”].
- **Documentary verification** using one document from each list in Appendix A. Documents must be current (or, for address documents, dated within the last three months) and must be originals or certified copies. Where we see documents remotely, we use [Insert method, e.g. an identity verification app with liveness checks, or a video call].
**4.3 Checking documents.** Staff check that documents appear genuine and unaltered, that photographs are a true likeness, that details are consistent across documents and with what the customer has told us, and that the customer’s behaviour does not suggest impersonation.
**4.4 Customers who cannot provide standard evidence.** Some customers, including people in vulnerable circumstances, may not have standard documents. We will not exclude them unreasonably. [Insert name or role] may approve alternative evidence (for example a letter from a care home manager, a benefits award letter, or a letter from a recognised professional), and records the reason.
**4.5 Purpose and intended nature.** We record why the customer is using our service and what we expect their activity to look like, so that we can recognise activity that is unusual for them.
**4.6 Sanctions screening.** Every customer (and, where relevant, beneficial owners and third-party payers) is screened against the UK sanctions list before onboarding, as our Anti-Money Laundering Policy requires. Results are recorded in the Sanctions Screening Log.
**4.7 Third-party payments.** Where someone other than the customer pays us, or funds a deposit or transaction, we identify the payer, establish their relationship to the customer, and record a credible explanation. We do not accept third-party payments where we cannot do so.

# 5. Customer risk assessment
We give every customer a risk rating – **low**, **standard** or **high** – when we onboard them, using the factors in our business-wide risk assessment, including:
- the customer (for example whether they are a PEP, have an unusual profile, or are acting for someone else);
- the product or service and the value of the transaction;
- how we deal with the customer (face-to-face, remote, or through an introducer);
- geography (where the customer lives or where funds come from); and
- any warning signs identified during onboarding.
The rating decides whether SDD, standard CDD or EDD applies and how closely we monitor the relationship. It is recorded in the CDD Register (in the Customer Due Diligence Checklist workbook) and reviewed when circumstances change. Each customer’s checks are recorded on the CDD Checklist and, where enhanced due diligence applies, the EDD Checklist.

# 6. Simplified due diligence
NOTE: SDD is a concept in the MLRs. If your firm is outside the MLRs, delete this section – you already apply proportionate measures under section 3.
Where our business-wide risk assessment shows that a relationship or transaction presents a low risk of money laundering and terrorist financing, we may apply simplified due diligence (regulation 37 of the MLRs). This means adjusting the extent, timing or type of CDD measures – it never means doing no CDD at all. We still identify every customer and monitor the relationship. We record why SDD was appropriate, and we move the customer to standard or enhanced measures if anything suggests the risk is higher. We never apply SDD where we suspect money laundering or terrorist financing, or where any EDD trigger in section 7 is present.

# 7. Enhanced due diligence
NOTE: Firms within the MLRs must apply EDD in the situations regulation 33 requires. Firms outside the MLRs should keep this section as firm policy but may simplify the measures in 7.2 to match their risks.
**7.1 When EDD applies.** We apply EDD where:
- a customer, beneficial owner or third-party payer is a **PEP**, or a family member or known close associate of a PEP. For domestic (UK) PEPs, the starting point is a lower level of risk than for foreign PEPs, and the EDD we apply reflects that unless other risk factors are present;
- the customer or transaction involves a **high-risk third country** – since 30 June 2026 this mandatory requirement covers jurisdictions subject to a FATF “call for action”. Links to jurisdictions under increased FATF monitoring are assessed as part of our risk rating;
- a transaction is **unusually complex or unusually large**, or part of an unusual pattern of transactions, or has no apparent economic or legal purpose;
- we have assessed the customer as **high risk** for any other reason (for example unexplained third-party involvement, inconsistent information, or adverse media); or
- we have identified information that suggests a higher risk of fraud.
Dealing with a customer remotely is a risk factor we take into account, not an automatic trigger for EDD; we manage it through the verification methods in section 4.2.
**7.2 EDD measures.** Depending on the risk, EDD may include:
- additional verification of identity or address from further independent sources;
- establishing and verifying the **source of funds** (for example bank statements, payslips, a completion statement for a property sale, or a solicitor’s letter for an inheritance) and, where appropriate, the **source of wealth**;
- obtaining **senior management approval** before establishing or continuing the relationship (always required for PEPs, for firms within the MLRs);
- more frequent and closer monitoring of the relationship; and
- reviewing the relationship at least [Insert frequency].
**7.3 Recording decisions.** All EDD decisions, the evidence obtained and the approver’s name are recorded on the EDD Checklist and in the CDD Register.

# 8. Where CDD cannot be completed
If we cannot complete the CDD this policy requires, we must not establish the relationship, carry out the transaction, or submit the application. Where a relationship already exists, we will take steps to end it. In each case, the member of staff must consider whether the circumstances give rise to knowledge or suspicion of money laundering, terrorist financing or fraud, and if so report it to the MLRO. We will not tell the customer anything that could tip them off.
We will not refuse or end relationships simply because a customer falls into a higher-risk category (for example because they are a PEP) without assessing their individual risk, and we will be mindful of our obligations to treat customers fairly and not to discriminate.

We monitor each relationship to make sure activity is consistent with what we know about the customer and to keep CDD information up to date. We refresh CDD:
- when there is a material change in the customer’s circumstances or activity;
- when we have doubts about information previously obtained;
- when a customer’s risk rating changes;
- for high-risk customers, at least [Insert frequency, e.g. annually]; and for other customers, [Insert trigger or frequency, e.g. before any new product or transaction, or at least every three years for ongoing relationships].

# 11. Records
We keep, in the CDD Checklist, EDD Checklist, CDD Register and customer files:
- copies of, or references to, the evidence used to verify identity, the date and method of verification and the name of the person who carried it out;
- the customer risk rating and the reasons for it;
- records of SDD and EDD decisions, including approvals;
- records of reliance, where used; and
- screening results.
We keep these records for **five years** after the end of the business relationship or the date of an occasional transaction (regulation 40 of the MLRs, for firms within the MLRs; firm policy for others), then delete them securely unless we must keep them longer. Records are stored securely and can be produced promptly to the FCA, HMRC or law enforcement on lawful request.
