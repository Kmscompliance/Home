# 2. What is a complaint?
We use the FCA’s definition. A complaint is **any oral or written expression of dissatisfaction, whether justified or not**, from, or on behalf of, a person about the provision of, or failure to provide, a financial service (or a redress determination), which alleges that the person has suffered, or may suffer, financial loss, material distress or material inconvenience.
In practice this means:
- A complaint can be made in **any way** – by telephone, email, letter, web form, social media message or in person. The customer does not need to use the word “complaint”, fill in a form, or put it in writing.
- A complaint can be made by the customer or by someone acting on their behalf (for example a family member, a person with power of attorney, a solicitor or a claims management company). We will confirm the representative’s authority proportionately and without creating unnecessary barriers.
- If there is any doubt about whether something is a complaint, we treat it as one.
- We do not charge for handling complaints, and we never ask a customer to give up their right to complain as a condition of settling a dispute.
**Eligible complainants.** The Ombudsman can consider complaints from “eligible complainants” as defined in DISP 2.7. These include consumers, micro-enterprises and certain small businesses, charities and trusts that meet DISP thresholds, and guarantors. We handle complaints from any person fairly, but the DISP time limits and Ombudsman referral rights described below apply to eligible complainants.
**There is no firm-imposed deadline for complaining.** We do not refuse to consider a complaint because the customer has not complained within a set period after the event. The only time limits are the Ombudsman’s own time limits (see section 6).

# 4. How we handle a complaint
The same process applies to every complaint, whatever channel it arrives through.
**Step 1 – Record.** The person who receives the complaint records it in our Complaints Register (Appendix B) by the end of the next business day, and passes it to [Insert name or role of the person who handles complaints]. We record the date the complaint was received – the DISP time limits run from that date, not from the date it reaches the complaints handler.
**Step 2 – Acknowledge.** We send the complainant a prompt written acknowledgement, normally within [Insert number – we suggest no more than 5] business days, giving the name or role of the person handling it, a copy of (or link to) our complaints summary, and what they can expect next (DISP 1.6.1R).
**Step 3 – Resolve quickly where we can.** If we resolve the complaint to the complainant’s satisfaction by the close of the **third business day** after the day we received it, we may send a **summary resolution communication** instead of a final response. It must tell the complainant that we consider the complaint resolved, that they can refer it to the Ombudsman if they later become dissatisfied, and give the Ombudsman’s website address (DISP 1.5).
**Step 4 – Investigate.** Where the complaint is not resolved at Step 3, the investigation will be carried out competently, diligently and impartially by someone who was not directly involved in the matter complained about wherever that is possible in our firm (DISP 1.4.1R). We will:
- identify all the points the complainant has raised, and check our understanding with them where it is unclear;
- gather the relevant evidence (customer file, call recordings, emails, system notes, third-party information);
- assess the complaint fairly, consistently and promptly, taking into account relevant FCA rules and guidance, the law and any relevant Ombudsman decisions; and
- keep the complainant informed of progress.
**Step 5 – Put things right.** Where we uphold a complaint, in full or in part, we will offer fair redress. This aims to put the customer back in the position they would have been in if things had not gone wrong, and may include: correcting the error; refunding fees or charges; compensating for financial loss (with interest where appropriate); compensating for distress and inconvenience; correcting records (including credit files, where relevant); and an apology. We pay agreed redress promptly – within [Insert number, e.g. 28] days of the complainant accepting it.
**Step 6 – Final response.** We send a written **final response** that accepts or rejects the complaint with clear reasons, sets out any redress offered, tells the complainant they may refer the complaint to the Ombudsman within six months, gives the Ombudsman’s website address and includes (or links to) the Ombudsman’s standard explanatory leaflet, and states whether we consent to the Ombudsman considering a complaint that is outside its time limits (DISP 1.6.2R and DISP 1 Annex 3R).
**Step 7 – Close and learn.** We record the outcome, redress and root cause in the Complaints Register and consider whether other customers could have been affected in the same way (see section 9).
**Complaints involving another firm.** If we believe another firm (for example a lender, insurer or product provider) is solely or jointly responsible for what is complained about, we will promptly forward the complaint, or the relevant part of it, to that firm in writing, tell the complainant why and give them the other firm’s contact details (DISP 1.7). Where we are jointly responsible, we will still deal with our part of the complaint.

# 6. The Financial Ombudsman Service
If a complainant is unhappy with our final response, or we have not resolved the complaint within the time limits in section 5, an eligible complainant can refer it to the Financial Ombudsman Service free of charge. The Ombudsman is independent of us and its decisions, if accepted by the complainant, are binding on us.
**Time limits.** The complainant must normally refer the complaint to the Ombudsman within **six months** of the date of our final response. The Ombudsman also generally cannot consider a complaint made more than **six years** after the event complained about or, if later, more than **three years** after the complainant became aware (or ought reasonably to have become aware) that they had cause to complain (DISP 2.8). The Ombudsman can consider a complaint outside these limits if we consent or there are exceptional circumstances.
**Contact details:** Financial Ombudsman Service, Exchange Tower, London E14 9SR. Telephone 0800 023 4567. Website: www.financial-ombudsman.org.uk.
**Award limits.** The Ombudsman can award compensation up to its award limit, which is adjusted each year in line with inflation. For complaints referred on or after 1 April 2026 about acts or omissions on or after 1 April 2019, the limit is £455,000. [Check the current limit each April and update this sentence.]
**Changes to Ombudsman procedures.** Following the FCA and Ombudsman policy statement on modernising the redress system (August 2026), new grounds on which the Ombudsman may dismiss complaints, and a clarified “fair and reasonable” test, apply to complaints referred to the Ombudsman on or after 1 October 2026.

# 8. Treating complainants fairly
- We treat every complainant with courtesy, respect and patience, and communicate in plain language.
- We take account of characteristics of vulnerability (see our Customer Vulnerability Policy) and make reasonable adjustments, such as extra time, a single point of contact, communication through a trusted third party, or alternative formats.
- We do not discourage customers from complaining, and we do not treat customers less favourably because they have complained.
- We will not continue with any action that is the subject of the complaint (for example charging a disputed fee) where doing so could cause further harm while we investigate.
- We will not ask a complainant to sign a confidentiality clause or waive their right to go to the Ombudsman as a condition of redress.

# 11. Data protection complaints
Since 19 June 2026, the Data Protection Act 2018 (as amended by the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025) has required us, as a data controller, to have a process for handling complaints about how we use personal data. We will acknowledge any data protection complaint within 30 days, take appropriate steps to respond without undue delay, and tell the complainant the outcome. A data protection complaint may also be a DISP complaint; where it is, we handle it under both processes and meet the shorter deadline. Complainants can also complain to the Information Commissioner’s Office (www.ico.org.uk).
