# 2. Our monitoring approach
Our monitoring is **risk-based and proportionate**. We focus most effort on the areas where customers could suffer the most harm and where things are most likely to go wrong. As a [Insert description, e.g. “small firm with two staff”] we carry out most monitoring in-house, supported by [Insert: an external compliance consultant / no external support – delete as appropriate].
We use five types of check:
TABLE: 25,75
| Type of check | What it involves |
| File reviews | Reviewing a sample of customer files end-to-end against the rules and our procedures (for example information given, suitability or affordability where relevant, records, and outcome). |
| Outcome and MI reviews | Analysing management information (MI) – for example complaints, cancellations, arrears, call data or customer feedback – to see whether customers are getting good outcomes, including customers with characteristics of vulnerability. |
| Thematic reviews | A deeper review of one topic across the business (for example how we support customers in financial difficulty). |
| Control checks | Testing that a control operates as designed (for example that every financial promotion was approved before publication, or that reconciliations are completed on time). |
| Regulatory change reviews | Checking that new or changed FCA rules and guidance have been identified, assessed and implemented. |
**Sampling.** For file reviews we select at least [Insert number, e.g. 5] files, or [Insert percentage, e.g. 10%] of files, per review period, whichever is greater, weighted towards higher-risk cases (for example new staff, vulnerable customers, complaints, large transactions). Where there are fewer files than this, we review them all. Where a review finds problems, we increase the sample.

# 3. How we rate risk and set frequency
Each monitoring area is given a risk rating, which sets how often we check it. We review the ratings at least annually and whenever monitoring, complaints or regulatory change suggest the risk has changed.

# 5. How we carry out monitoring
**Step 1 – Plan.** At the start of each [Insert period, e.g. quarter], [Insert owner] confirms, using the Compliance Monitoring Schedule, which checks are due under the schedule and any additional reviews prompted by complaints, incidents or regulatory change.
**Step 2 – Select the sample.** Files are selected as described in section 2, with the selection method recorded.
**Step 3 – Review using a checklist.** Each check uses a standard checklist linked to the relevant rules, so that reviews are consistent and can be repeated. The result for each file is recorded in the File Review Record, and checklists are kept at [Insert location].
**Step 4 – Record findings and evidence.** Findings are recorded against each checklist question, with evidence kept in the Monitoring Evidence Folder [Insert location]. Each finding is rated: **Red** (breach of a rule or risk of customer harm), **Amber** (weakness that could lead to a breach) or **Green** (satisfactory).
**Step 5 – Act on findings.** Every Red or Amber finding is entered in the Corrective Action Log with an owner and deadline (section 8).
**Step 6 – Escalate serious matters.** Serious issues are escalated immediately (section 7), not held for the next report.
**Step 7 – Retain records.** Monitoring records are kept for at least [Insert period, e.g. six years].
**Independence.** Where possible, reviews are carried out by someone who did not do the work being reviewed. Where our size makes that impossible, [Insert how you get independent challenge – e.g. an annual review by an external compliance consultant].

# 7. Breaches, escalation and notifications
Any member of staff who identifies a possible breach of FCA rules or our policies must report it immediately to [Insert name or role]. We record every breach in our **Compliance Breach Log**, including what happened, the cause, whether customers were harmed, the remedial action and whether the FCA was notified.
We consider whether each breach must be notified to the FCA. Under Principle 11 and SUP 15.3 we must tell the FCA promptly about anything it would reasonably expect notice of, including significant rule breaches, matters that could affect our ability to meet the Threshold Conditions, and significant events such as fraud or serious operational failures. SM&CR firms must also notify Conduct Rules breaches as SUP 15.11 requires. Where we are unsure, we will notify.
WATCH: The FCA’s new operational incident and third-party reporting rules (PS26/2, March 2026) will apply to almost all FCA-regulated firms from 18 March 2027, introducing standard incident report requirements. Update this section, and your incident procedure, before that date.
Serious issues – such as systemic mis-selling, harm to vulnerable customers, a suspected fraud, or a failure of client money controls – are escalated to [Insert name of senior manager] the same day and may be referred to our external compliance adviser.

# 8. Corrective action and continuous improvement
Identifying a problem is the start of the process, not the end. For every Red or Amber finding, breach or significant complaint theme:
- **Record** the issue in the Corrective Action Log: what was found, who found it, the rule or principle affected and the proposed fix.
- **Assign** an owner and a deadline proportionate to the risk ([Insert standard, e.g. Red within 30 days, Amber within 90 days]).
- **Fix the root cause**, not just the symptom – for example retraining, changing a process or script, updating a policy, or changing a system.
- **Put customers right** where they have been harmed or disadvantaged, including customers who have not complained.
- **Verify and close** the action only when [Insert name or role] is satisfied that the root cause has been addressed and the fix has worked (usually by re-testing).
- **Look for trends** across the Log each quarter, and report them in the Compliance Monitoring Report.

# 9. Review of this plan
This plan is reviewed at least annually, and also when we add a new product, activity, channel or significant partner; when staff roles change; after a significant complaint, breach or FCA communication; and when relevant FCA rules change. The review considers whether risk ratings and frequencies remain right, whether any area is missing, and whether monitoring is finding issues before customers are harmed. Changes are approved by [Insert: the board / the directors / the business owner].

# 10. Summary
This plan is how we make sure our policies work in practice and our customers get good outcomes. Our aim is not simply to tick boxes, but to find problems early, fix them properly and keep improving.
