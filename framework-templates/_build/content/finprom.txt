# 2. Regulatory framework
- **Section 21 of the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 (FSMA)** makes it a criminal offence for a person, in the course of business, to communicate an invitation or inducement to engage in investment activity (which includes entering into regulated credit agreements, insurance and mortgages, and investing) unless they are FCA-authorised, the content is approved by an authorised person with the right permission, or an exemption applies.
- **The section 21 approver regime.** Since 7 February 2024 (PS23/13), an authorised firm must have specific FCA permission before approving financial promotions for unauthorised persons, unless an exemption applies (for example promotions of its own appointed representatives or, in some cases, relating to its own products). We [Insert: do not approve promotions for unauthorised persons / hold the section 21 approver permission – delete as appropriate].
- **The Consumer Duty (PRIN 2A)** – in particular the consumer understanding outcome (PRIN 2A.5), which requires communications to meet customers’ information needs, be likely to be understood by the customers they are aimed at, and equip them to make effective, timely and properly informed decisions. The Duty applies to communications that are not financial promotions as well as to those that are.
- **Activity-specific rules** – set out in section 7.
- **FG24/1**, the FCA’s finalised guidance on financial promotions on social media (March 2024), which replaced FG15/4 and covers the use of social media and influencers.
- **Consumer protection law** – the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 (which replaced the Consumer Protection from Unfair Trading Regulations 2008 from April 2025 and is enforced by the CMA and Trading Standards), and the Advertising Standards Authority’s CAP and BCAP Codes.
- **The Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations 2003** – for email, text and telephone marketing (see our Data Processing Policy).

# 3. What is a financial promotion?
A financial promotion is any communication, in the course of business, that invites or induces someone to engage in investment activity – for our purposes, to take out or use a regulated product or service. It does not have to mention a price or rate to be a promotion: an image, a short video, a slogan, a sponsored post or a comment from a member of staff can all be promotions.
Examples include: “Finance available – apply now”; an advert showing a monthly payment; a social media post about our service; a comparison of products on our website; and a salesperson describing the benefits of a product in a conversation.
**Communications that are not promotions** (for example account letters, statements, arrears letters and complaint responses) must still be clear, fair and not misleading and meet the consumer understanding outcome.
**Every promotion must comply on its own.** Each stage of the customer journey, and each individual social media post, must comply by itself – for example, an advert cannot rely on information that the customer would only see after clicking through.

# 5. Core standards for every promotion
Every promotion and customer communication must:
- be **fair, clear and not misleading**, judged from the perspective of the customers it is aimed at;
- give a **balanced** impression: benefits must not be emphasised without an equally prominent indication of any relevant risks, costs or limitations;
- make key information (for example costs, risks and the nature of our role) **prominent** – it must not be hidden in small print, footnotes or behind links;
- use **plain language** and explain any technical terms;
- **identify the firm** clearly, including our name and, where required, our FCA status;
- not **exploit** customers’ behavioural biases, lack of knowledge or vulnerability, or use pressure selling (for example false urgency or countdown timers);
- make only claims that are **accurate and can be evidenced** at the time the promotion is used;
- be **consistent** with the product or service, its target market and our fair value assessment; and
- be **tested** for understanding where appropriate (for example key customer journeys), in line with the Consumer Duty.

# 7. Activity-specific rules

OPT: 7A | Credit broking firms | your firm carries on, or is applying for, the credit broking permission.
**7A.1 The rules.** Promotions relating to credit must comply with **CONC 3**, which applies to credit brokers as well as lenders. In particular:
- **Broker status.** Promotions must indicate the extent to which we are acting as a credit broker and that we are not a lender, and whether we work with a limited number of lenders. Any fee the customer must pay us must be disclosed.
- **Representative example and APR.** If a promotion includes an interest rate or any amount relating to the cost of credit, it must include a representative example with the information CONC 3.5 requires, shown together and more prominently than the trigger information. The representative APR must be the rate at or below which we reasonably expect at least 51% of agreements entered into as a result of the promotion to be made.
- **Prohibited claims.** Promotions must not suggest credit is available regardless of the customer’s financial circumstances or credit history, or describe credit as “pre-approved” or “guaranteed” unless the rules allow it.
- **High-cost short-term credit.** Promotions of high-cost short-term credit must include the prescribed risk warning. [Delete if you do not broker high-cost short-term credit.]
**7A.2 Commission and point-of-sale promotions.** Where we are also a retailer, finance offers presented alongside goods (for example “£199 a month”) are credit promotions and must meet CONC 3. Our promotions must not give a misleading impression of whether we are independent or of the range of lenders we use.
**7A.3 Deferred payment credit.** Since 15 July 2026, deferred payment credit (“buy now, pay later”) offered by third-party lenders has been regulated, but broking of deferred payment credit is not itself regulated. Promotions we communicate for regulated deferred payment credit must still comply with section 21 of FSMA. [Delete if not applicable.]

OPT: 7D | Firms providing credit information services or credit references | your firm provides credit information services and/or credit references.
**7D.1 The rules.** Promotions of credit information services must comply with **CONC 3** and the Consumer Duty. In particular they must not overstate what the service can achieve – for example claiming that it will “fix” or “repair” a credit rating, remove accurate information, or guarantee access to credit.
**7D.2 Free access and subscriptions.** Promotions must make clear that individuals have a statutory right to obtain their credit file, and must not give the impression that they need to pay to see it. OPT: 7E | Peer-to-peer lending platforms | your firm operates, or is applying to operate, an electronic system in relation to lending.
**7E.1 Investor promotions.** Peer-to-peer agreements are **restricted mass market investments**. Under **COBS 4.12A**, promotions to retail investors must:
- include the prescribed risk warning, prominently: “Don’t invest unless you’re prepared to lose money. This is a high-risk investment. You may not be able to access your money easily and are unlikely to be protected if something goes wrong. Take 2 mins to learn more.” – with the link to the fuller risk summary;
- not offer incentives to invest (for example cash bonuses or refer-a-friend rewards); and
- only be communicated to investors who have been categorised as restricted, certified high net worth or certified sophisticated investors, and – for direct offer promotions – who have passed an appropriateness assessment, with a 24-hour cooling-off period for first-time investors and a personalised risk warning.
**7E.2 Returns and past performance.** Any target or past returns must be presented fairly with defaults and losses, and must not be more prominent than the risk warning.
**7E.3 Borrower promotions.** Promotions to consumer borrowers must comply with **CONC 3**, including the representative example requirements where a rate or cost is stated.

# 8. Targeting and vulnerable customers
Promotions are aimed only at customers within the target market for the product or service. We do not target customers who are likely to be harmed by the product – for example, we do not direct credit promotions at customers we know to be in financial difficulty, and we do not use data about customers’ vulnerability to target them with promotions. We consider how customers with characteristics of vulnerability are likely to understand each promotion, in line with our Customer Vulnerability Policy.
