# Introduction
This plan sets out how [Insert firm legal name] (“we”, “the firm”) meets and embeds the FCA’s Consumer Duty across its business. Our regulated activities are: [Specify the regulated activities / permissions your firm holds or is applying for]. Our retail customers are: [Insert a short description of your target customers].
The Duty sets a higher standard of care than the previous Principles 6 and 7 for our dealings with retail customers. It requires us to put customers’ interests at the heart of our business, to focus on the outcomes they actually experience, and to be able to evidence that those outcomes are good – including for customers with characteristics of vulnerability.
The Duty is made up of:
- **the Consumer Principle** (Principle 12): a firm must act to deliver good outcomes for retail customers;
- **the cross-cutting rules** (PRIN 2A.2): we must act in good faith towards retail customers; avoid causing foreseeable harm to them; and enable and support them to pursue their financial objectives; and
- **the four outcomes**: products and services (PRIN 2A.3); price and value (PRIN 2A.4); consumer understanding (PRIN 2A.5); and consumer support (PRIN 2A.6),
supported by requirements on governance, culture, monitoring of outcomes and an annual board report. We also follow the FCA’s non-Handbook guidance on the Duty (FG22/5) and the FCA’s published findings and good practice.

# 1. What each part of the Duty means for us
## Acting in good faith
We deal with customers honestly, fairly and openly, consistent with their reasonable expectations. We do not exploit customers’ lack of knowledge, behavioural biases or vulnerability, and we do not use practices (for example hidden charges, confusing choice architecture or unreasonable barriers to leaving) that work against customers’ interests.
## Avoiding foreseeable harm
We think ahead about how our products, services, communications and processes could harm customers – including through our own actions and through features that only affect some groups – and we design them to prevent that harm. Where harm happens, we act to put things right and stop it happening again.
## Enabling and supporting customers
We make sure customers have what they need to make good decisions – the right information at the right time, products that meet their needs, and support that is easy to access – and we remove unnecessary barriers.
## Applying the Duty proportionately
The Duty applies in a way that reflects our size, our role and the nature of our products. As a [Insert description, e.g. “small firm with one director”], we meet it through simple, well-documented processes that are built into our day-to-day work, rather than through large-scale programmes.

# 2. Our role in the distribution chain

OPT: 2A | Credit broking firms | your firm carries on, or is applying for, the credit broking permission.
**2A.1 Our role.** We are a **distributor** of credit products manufactured by the lenders on our panel. If we charge customers a fee, we are also the **manufacturer** of our own broking service.
**2A.2 Products and services.** We obtain from each lender the information we need about its products’ target market, characteristics and distribution strategy, and we only introduce customers for whom the product is likely to be suitable. We share information with lenders about the outcomes we see (for example complaints and cancellations) when they ask, and proactively where we identify harm.
**2A.3 Price and value.** We assess the value of our own fees. We consider the effect of the commission we receive on the overall value of the product to the customer, and we obtain lenders’ fair value assessments. Commission structures must not create an incentive to introduce customers to more expensive credit.
**2A.4 Understanding and support.** Customers must understand that we are a broker, the extent of our lender panel, whether and how we are paid commission, and the key features and costs of the credit. We make it easy for customers to contact the lender after the agreement is made.
ENDOPT: 2A | Credit broking firms
OPT: 2B | Debt adjusting and debt counselling firms | your firm carries on, or is applying for, debt adjusting and/or debt counselling.
**2B.1 Our role.** We are the **manufacturer** of the debt advice and debt solution services we provide.
**2B.2 Products and services.** Our target market is customers in financial difficulty, many of whom have characteristics of vulnerability. Our services must deliver solutions that are sustainable and genuinely in customers’ interests, and we consider free-to-client and statutory alternatives in every case.
**2B.3 Price and value.** We assess whether our fees are reasonable relative to the benefit customers receive, taking account of the availability of free debt advice and the effect of fees on how quickly debts are repaid.
**2B.4 Understanding and support.** Customers must understand the consequences of each option (including the effect on their credit file and assets), the fees they will pay, and that free debt advice is available. We support customers when their circumstances change, including through Breathing Space.
ENDOPT: 2B | Debt adjusting and debt counselling firms
OPT: 2C | Debt collecting and debt administration firms | your firm carries on, or is applying for, debt collecting and/or debt administration.
**2C.1 Our role.** We provide collection or administration services on behalf of [Insert: lenders / debt owners]. We are responsible for the outcomes our own activities cause for the customers we deal with, even though the credit product was manufactured by another firm.
**2C.2 Products and services.** Our collection strategies and processes are designed to avoid foreseeable harm, particularly to customers in financial difficulty.
**2C.3 Price and value.** Any charges we add to customers’ accounts, or that our clients add on our recommendation, must be fair and reflect costs reasonably incurred.
**2C.4 Understanding and support.** Customers must understand who we are, what they owe and why, and what options are available to them. OPT: 2D | Firms providing credit information services or credit references | your firm provides credit information services and/or credit references.
**2D.1 Our role.** Where we provide credit information services directly to consumers, we are the **manufacturer** of those services. Where we supply credit references to lenders, the accuracy of our data directly affects outcomes for retail customers, even though they are not our direct customers.
**2D.2 Products and services.** Our services must meet a genuine need and must not encourage customers to pay for things they can get free (for example their statutory credit report).
**2D.3 Price and value.** We assess whether subscriptions and paid services provide fair value, including the non-financial costs of cancelling.
**2D.4 Understanding and support.** Customers must understand what a credit score is and what affects it, what our service can and cannot do, and how to dispute information. Disputes and corrections must be handled quickly and fairly.

OPT: 2E | Peer-to-peer lending platforms | your firm operates, or is applying to operate, an electronic system in relation to lending.
**2E.1 Our role.** We are the **manufacturer** of our platform’s services for both investors and borrowers.
**2E.2 Products and services.** We define separate target markets for investors (for example restricted, high net worth or sophisticated investors who can bear losses) and borrowers, and design our credit assessment, pricing and loan monitoring to avoid foreseeable harm to both.
**2E.3 Price and value.** We assess the value of our fees to investors against realistic, risk-adjusted returns, and to borrowers against the cost of comparable credit.
**2E.4 Understanding and support.** Investors must understand the risk of loss, the lack of FSCS protection and how easily they can access their money. Borrowers must understand their obligations. Both must receive support if things change, including if the platform winds down.

OPT: 2F | Consumer credit lenders | your firm enters into, or is applying to enter into, regulated credit agreements as lender (including deferred payment credit / “buy now, pay later”).
**2F.1 Our role.** We are the **manufacturer** of our credit products. Where brokers or retailers introduce customers, they are our **distributors**.
**2F.2 Products and services.** We run a product approval process that defines the target market, tests how products perform for different customer groups (including in stressed scenarios) and sets a distribution strategy. We give our distributors the information they need and monitor the outcomes customers receive through each channel.
**2F.3 Price and value.** Our fair value assessment considers interest, fees and charges (including default charges), the commission we pay to distributors, and whether any groups of customers – for example those who fall into arrears – receive poor value.
**2F.4 Understanding and support.** Customers must understand the total cost, the consequences of missing payments and their rights. We support customers in financial difficulty in line with CONC 7, and make it easy to repay early, get statements and complain.
ENDOPT: 2F | Consumer credit lenders
OPT: 2G | Firms exercising a lender’s rights and duties | your firm exercises, or has the right to exercise, the lender’s rights and duties under regulated credit agreements.
**2G.1 Our role.** We hold agreements that were manufactured by another firm. As the owner, we are responsible for the outcomes customers receive from the date we acquire the agreements, and the Duty applies to these closed products.
**2G.2 Products and services.** We review each portfolio on acquisition for features that could cause harm (for example high ongoing charges or unsustainable repayment arrangements).
**2G.3 Price and value.** We assess the fair value of the agreements we hold on an ongoing basis and change features that do not offer fair value where we can.
**2G.4 Understanding and support.** Customers must understand who we are and how the transfer affects them. We provide accessible support and treat customers in arrears with forbearance.
ENDOPT: 2G | Firms exercising a lender’s rights and duties
OPT: 2H | Consumer hire firms | your firm hires out goods under regulated consumer hire agreements.

**2H.2 Products and services.** Our hire products are designed for a defined target market, and we consider whether hire (rather than purchase) meets the needs of the customers we serve.
**2H.3 Price and value.** Our fair value assessment covers the total cost of hire compared with alternatives, and end-of-hire, damage and excess-use charges.
**2H.4 Understanding and support.** Customers must understand that they will not own the goods, the total cost and any end-of-hire charges. We provide support with maintenance, early termination and arrears.

# 3. Products and services outcome
- We define the **target market** for each product or service we manufacture – the customers whose needs, characteristics and objectives it is designed to meet, including any customers with characteristics of vulnerability – and any customers it is not suitable for.
- We **approve** new products and significant changes through a documented process that is signed off by [Insert name or role], including testing how the product is likely to perform for customers.
- As a distributor, we obtain the information we need from manufacturers and **only distribute** to customers in the target market.
- We **review** each product and service at least [Insert frequency] and when outcome data or complaints suggest a problem, and take action where it is not delivering good outcomes.

# 4. Price and value outcome
- We carry out a documented **fair value assessment** for every product and service we manufacture, comparing the total price customers pay (including fees, charges and non-financial costs such as time and effort) with the benefits they receive.
- We consider whether any **groups of customers** – including those with characteristics of vulnerability – receive worse value.
- As a distributor, we obtain manufacturers’ fair value assessments and make sure our own **remuneration** does not undermine value.
- We **review** fair value at least annually, and sooner if prices, costs or outcome data change. Where a product does not provide fair value, we act – for example by changing the price or features, withdrawing it, or providing redress.

# 5. Consumer understanding outcome
- Our communications are clear, relevant, well-timed and in plain language, and give customers the information they need to make decisions, including key risks, costs and consequences of inaction.
- We apply at least the same care to post-sale communications (for example how to cancel or complain) as to sales communications.
- We **test** key communications (for example with a sample of customers or through [Insert method]) and use the results and outcome data to improve them.
- Where we deal with customers one-to-one, we check understanding and tailor explanations to the customer’s needs.
- We make communications **accessible**, including reasonable adjustments for disabled customers.

# 6. Consumer support outcome
- Customers can contact us easily through [Insert channels], and it is **as easy to get help, switch, cancel or complain as it was to buy**.
- We avoid unreasonable barriers (“sludge”), such as long waiting times, complicated processes to cancel, or requiring customers to use a channel that does not suit them.
- We provide appropriate support to customers with characteristics of vulnerability, in line with our Customer Vulnerability Policy.
- We handle complaints fairly and use them to improve, in line with our Complaints Procedure.
