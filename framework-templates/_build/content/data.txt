# 3. Data protection principles
We comply with the principles in Article 5 of the UK GDPR. Personal data must be:
- processed **lawfully, fairly and transparently**;
- collected for **specified, explicit and legitimate purposes** and not used in ways incompatible with those purposes;
- **adequate, relevant and limited** to what is necessary;
- **accurate** and, where necessary, kept up to date;
- kept for **no longer than necessary**; and
- kept **secure**.
We are responsible for, and must be able to demonstrate, compliance with these principles (**accountability**). We keep a Record of Processing Activities (the ROPA tab in the Data Protection Registers workbook), carry out data protection impact assessments where required (section 12), and keep records of our decisions.

# 5. Lawful basis for processing
We only process personal data where we have a lawful basis under Article 6 of the UK GDPR. We identify and record the lawful basis for each purpose before we start processing. The bases we use are:
- **Contract** – where processing is necessary to perform a contract with the individual or to take steps at their request before entering into one (for example processing an application).
- **Legal obligation** – where we must process data to comply with the law (for example anti-money laundering checks, FCA record-keeping requirements, or tax law).
- **Legitimate interests** – where processing is necessary for our legitimate interests or those of a third party, and those interests are not overridden by the individual’s interests, rights and freedoms. We complete and record a legitimate interests assessment before relying on this basis.
- **Recognised legitimate interests** – the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 created a limited list of “recognised legitimate interests” (for example certain processing for crime prevention, safeguarding vulnerable individuals and responding to emergencies) for which a balancing test is not required. We will only rely on this basis where the processing clearly falls within the list, and we record our reasoning.
- **Consent** – where we ask for the individual’s freely given, specific, informed and unambiguous agreement (for example for some marketing). Consent must be as easy to withdraw as to give.
- **Vital interests** – only where processing is necessary to protect someone’s life.
**Special category and criminal offence data.** Information about health, ethnicity, religion, sex life or orientation, biometric or genetic data is **special category data**, and information about criminal convictions and offences is **criminal offence data**. We only process it where we also meet a condition in Article 9 or Article 10 of the UK GDPR and, where required, Schedule 1 of the Data Protection Act 2018. The conditions we rely on are: [Insert, e.g. explicit consent; the Schedule 1 condition for safeguarding the economic well-being of certain individuals; preventing or detecting unlawful acts]. Where we rely on a Schedule 1 condition we keep an **appropriate policy document** [Insert location]. Information about customers’ vulnerability is handled in line with our Customer Vulnerability Policy.

# 11. Personal data breaches
A personal data breach is a breach of security leading to the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorised disclosure of, or access to, personal data. Examples include sending an email to the wrong person, a lost laptop or a cyber attack.
- **Report immediately.** Anyone who discovers or suspects a breach must report it immediately to [Insert name of data protection lead], however minor it seems.
- **Contain and assess.** The data protection lead contains the breach and assesses the likely risk to individuals.
- **Notify the ICO.** Where a breach is likely to result in a risk to individuals’ rights and freedoms, we notify the ICO without undue delay and, where feasible, within **72 hours** of becoming aware of it.
- **Tell individuals.** Where a breach is likely to result in a high risk to individuals, we tell them without undue delay, explaining what happened and what they can do to protect themselves.
- **Consider the FCA.** We consider whether the breach must also be notified to the FCA under Principle 11 and SUP 15 (for example a significant cyber incident or a breach affecting many customers).
- **Record every breach** in our Data Breach Log, including those we do not report, with the facts, effects, remedial action and lessons learned.

Significant cyber incidents and data breaches may then need to be reported to the FCA using the new standard incident report. Update this section before that date.
