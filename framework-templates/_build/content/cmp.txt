The CMP is how we meet the requirement in SYSC 6.1.1R to establish, implement and maintain adequate policies and procedures sufficient to ensure compliance with our regulatory obligations, and to detect and minimise the risk of failing to meet them. It also supports our obligation under the Consumer Duty (PRIN 2A) to monitor, regularly review and act on the outcomes our customers receive, and our continuing ability to meet the Threshold Conditions.

# 2. Objectives
The objectives of our CMP are to:
- identify the rules and obligations that apply to our business (our “compliance universe”) and keep that list up to date;
- test, on a risk-based schedule, whether our controls work in practice and whether customers are getting good outcomes;
- identify breaches, weaknesses and poor outcomes early, and make sure they are fixed at the root and that affected customers are put right;
- give senior management accurate, timely information so they can oversee compliance and make decisions; and
- promote a culture in which everyone in the firm understands their compliance responsibilities and feels able to raise concerns.

# 3. Our compliance universe
Our compliance universe is the full set of requirements that apply to us. It is recorded in our Compliance Universe Register (in the Compliance Monitoring Tracker workbook) and reviewed at least annually and whenever the rules or our business change.
**3.1 Requirements that apply to every firm.** These include:
- the Threshold Conditions (COND), the Principles for Businesses (PRIN) and the Consumer Duty (PRIN 2A);
- Senior Management Arrangements, Systems and Controls (SYSC), including record keeping, outsourcing and conflicts of interest where they apply;
- the Senior Managers and Certification Regime and the Conduct Rules (COCON), for firms within the regime;
- the Fit and Proper test (FIT) and Training and Competence (TC, where it applies);
- Dispute Resolution: Complaints (DISP);
- the financial promotion restriction in section 21 of the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 and the rules for our activities;
- Supervision (SUP), including notifications and regulatory reporting;
- financial crime law and rules, including the Money Laundering Regulations where they apply, and UK sanctions; and
- data protection law (UK GDPR, the Data Protection Act 2018 and the Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations).

# 4. How the programme works
**4.1 Risk-based approach.** We prioritise monitoring according to the risk of harm to customers and the market, and the likelihood of things going wrong. We take into account the complexity of each activity, volumes, the vulnerability of our customers, complaints and past findings, and the FCA’s current priorities for our sector.
**4.2 Monitoring plan.** Our Compliance Monitoring Plan sets out the areas we check, the risk rating, what we check, how often and who is responsible. It is approved by [Insert: the board / the directors / the business owner] each year.
**4.3 Monitoring activities.** Monitoring includes file reviews, reviews of outcome data and management information, thematic reviews, control testing and reviews of regulatory change. Each review uses a documented checklist and is evidenced.
**4.4 Consumer Duty outcome monitoring.** We collect and analyse data on the outcomes customers receive under each of the four Consumer Duty outcomes, including outcomes for different groups of customers such as those with characteristics of vulnerability. Our findings inform the annual Consumer Duty board report required by PRIN 2A.8.
**4.5 Breaches and corrective action.** Breaches are recorded in the Compliance Breach Log and assessed for FCA notification under Principle 11 and SUP 15. Every finding is tracked through the Corrective Action Log (in the Compliance Monitoring Tracker workbook) to closure, including putting affected customers right.
**4.6 Reporting and escalation.** Monitoring results are reported to [Insert: the board / the directors / the business owner] at least [Insert frequency]. Serious issues are escalated immediately.
