NOTE: **What the FCA looks for.** At authorisation, the FCA wants to see that you have identified the specific risks your business model creates (for customers, the market and your firm), that you have realistic controls for each, that you have enough financial and non-financial resources, and that you could wind down in an orderly way if you had to. A generic framework with no firm-specific risks is a common weakness – complete the Risk Assessment Log with your own risks.

# 1. Introduction
This Risk Management Framework (RMF) sets out how [Insert firm legal name] (“we”, “the firm”) identifies, assesses, controls, monitors and reports the risks arising from its business. Our regulated activities are: [Specify the regulated activities / permissions your firm holds or is applying for].
The RMF supports our obligations under SYSC 4.1.1R (robust governance arrangements and effective processes to identify, manage, monitor and report risks), SYSC 7 (risk control, where it applies to us), the Principles for Businesses, the Consumer Duty, and the Threshold Conditions – in particular the requirements to have appropriate resources (COND 2.4) and to be capable of being effectively supervised and managed in a sound and prudent way (COND 2.5).
It is proportionate to our size and complexity: [Insert short description, e.g. “we are a small firm with two directors and three staff”].

# 2. Objectives
Our risk management objectives are to:
- identify the risks that could cause harm to our customers, the market or the firm, including emerging risks;
- assess those risks consistently, and decide how much risk we are prepared to accept (our risk appetite);
- put in place and maintain controls that keep risks within appetite;
- monitor risks and controls, and report clearly to decision-makers;
- hold enough financial and non-financial resources to meet our obligations and absorb losses; and
- be able to wind down our business in an orderly way, without causing undue harm to customers, if we needed to.

# 5. The risk management process
**5.1 Identify.** We identify risks through: business planning; changes to products, channels or partners; complaints and customer feedback; compliance monitoring and incidents; regulatory developments and FCA publications; and a formal risk review at least [Insert frequency, e.g. quarterly]. All identified risks are recorded in our Risk Assessment Log (see Appendix A).
**5.2 Assess.** Each risk is scored for **impact** (1 to 5) and **likelihood** (1 to 5) before controls (inherent risk) and after controls (residual risk). Impact considers harm to customers, the market and the firm (financial, regulatory and reputational). Residual scores of 15 or more are **high**, 8 to 14 are **medium**, and 1 to 7 are **low**.
**5.3 Control.** For each risk we decide whether to reduce it (through controls such as policies, procedures, checks, systems, training, insurance or contractual protections), avoid it (by not doing the activity), transfer it (for example through insurance), or accept it within appetite. Each control has a named owner.
**5.4 Monitor.** Risk owners monitor their risks using key risk indicators [Insert examples, e.g. complaint volumes, arrears rates, overdue training, system downtime]. Controls are tested through our Compliance Monitoring Plan.
**5.5 Report and escalate.** A risk report is presented to [Insert: the board / the directors / the business owner] at least [Insert frequency], covering high and increasing risks, breaches of appetite, incidents, key risk indicators and actions. Any risk that moves outside appetite is escalated immediately to [Insert name].

# 6. Financial resources and wind-down planning
**6.1 Adequate financial resources.** We assess our financial resources at least [Insert frequency] to make sure we can meet our liabilities as they fall due, meet any prudential requirement for our activities (see section 8), absorb losses in a severe but plausible stress, and fund an orderly wind-down. We follow the FCA’s guidance on assessing adequate financial resources. Our current position and projections are set out in [Insert reference to your financial projections / business plan].
**6.2 Stress testing.** At least annually we test the effect of severe but plausible scenarios on our finances – for example [Insert scenarios, e.g. loss of our main lender relationship; a 50% fall in income; a large redress payment; the loss of a key person].
**6.3 Wind-down plan.** We maintain a wind-down plan that sets out the triggers that would lead us to consider winding down, the steps we would take, the time and cost involved, how we would treat customers (including any money or assets we hold for them) and how we would communicate with the FCA. It follows the FCA’s Wind-down Planning Guide and is reviewed at least annually. [Insert reference.]

# 7. Operational resilience, outsourcing and business continuity
- **Business continuity.** We maintain a business continuity plan covering loss of premises, systems, key suppliers and key people. It is tested at least annually. [Insert reference.]
- **Key person risk.** [Describe your arrangements, e.g. who covers if the director is unavailable.]
- **Outsourcing and suppliers.** We carry out due diligence on critical suppliers, have written agreements, monitor performance and have exit plans, as SYSC 8 requires. We remain responsible for outsourced activities.

# 8. Activity-specific risks and prudential requirements

OPT: 8A | Credit broking firms | your firm carries on, or is applying for, the credit broking permission.
**8A.1 Key risks.** Poor explanations or pressure selling at point of sale; inadequate commission disclosure; submission of inaccurate or fraudulent applications; dependence on a small number of lenders; introducer and staff misconduct; and redress exposure from historic commission arrangements (for example in motor finance).
**8A.2 Prudential requirements.** There is no specific capital requirement for credit brokers, but we must have adequate financial resources under the Threshold Conditions, including to meet potential redress liabilities. [State your approach, e.g. a minimum cash buffer.]
**8A.3 Resilience considerations.** Our income depends on lender relationships: we assess the impact of losing our main lender(s) and have a plan to replace them or wind down.
ENDOPT: 8A | Credit broking firms
OPT: 8B | Debt adjusting and debt counselling firms | your firm carries on, or is applying for, debt adjusting and/or debt counselling.
**8B.1 Key risks.** Unsuitable debt advice; customers’ financial difficulty or vulnerability being made worse; fees that do not offer fair value; lead generation risks; and loss or misuse of client money.
**8B.2 Prudential requirements.** Debt management firms are subject to the prudential rules in CONC 10, which set a minimum level of financial resources (based on the client money we hold). Where we hold client money, we must comply with CASS 11, including reconciliations and audit requirements.
**8B.3 Resilience considerations.** Our wind-down plan explains how customers’ plans would be transferred or closed, how client money would be returned or distributed, and how customers would be supported, if we stopped trading.
ENDOPT: 8B | Debt adjusting and debt counselling firms
OPT: 8C | Debt collecting and debt administration firms | your firm carries on, or is applying for, debt collecting and/or debt administration.
**8C.1 Key risks.** Unfair treatment of customers in arrears; collection activity that harms vulnerable customers; inaccurate data received from clients; pursuing the wrong person or statute-barred debts; and loss or delay of collected funds.
**8C.2 Prudential requirements.** There is no specific capital requirement, but we must have adequate financial resources under the Threshold Conditions. Where we hold money collected for clients, we protect it in [Insert: a designated client account / other arrangement] and reconcile it [Insert frequency].
**8C.3 Resilience considerations.** Our contracts with clients set out how accounts and collected money would be returned if we stopped trading.
ENDOPT: 8C | Debt collecting and debt administration firms
OPT: 8D | Firms providing credit information services or credit references | your firm provides credit information services and/or credit references.
**8D.1 Key risks.** Inaccurate data causing customers to be refused credit or charged more; data breaches and cyber attacks; misuse of data; misleading promotion of services; and failure to handle disputes and corrections properly.
**8D.2 Prudential requirements.** There is no specific capital requirement, but we must have adequate financial resources under the Threshold Conditions.
**8D.3 Resilience considerations.** Because lenders and consumers rely on our data, system availability and data integrity are critical. We set impact tolerances for outages and test our recovery arrangements.
ENDOPT: 8D | Firms providing credit information services or credit references
OPT: 8E | Peer-to-peer lending platforms | your firm operates, or is applying to operate, an electronic system in relation to lending.
**8E.1 Key risks.** Poor credit risk assessment or pricing; investors not understanding the risk of loss; conflicts between the platform’s interests and those of investors; loss of client money; and platform failure.
**8E.2 Prudential requirements.** We must meet the capital requirements in IPRU-INV 12 and hold client money in line with CASS 7.
**8E.3 Resilience considerations.** We must have arrangements to ensure that loans continue to be administered if the platform ceases to carry on its business (for example a back-up servicer). Our wind-down plan sets out how this would work and is tested at least annually.
ENDOPT: 8E | Peer-to-peer lending platforms
OPT: 8F | Consumer credit lenders | your firm enters into, or is applying to enter into, regulated credit agreements as lender.
**8F.1 Key risks.** Irresponsible lending; poor treatment of customers in arrears; credit losses higher than expected; funding risk (dependence on a single funder); fraud; errors in agreements or statutory notices that can make agreements unenforceable or require redress; and redress exposure from historic practices.
**8F.2 Prudential requirements.** There is no specific capital requirement for consumer credit lenders, but we must have adequate financial resources under the Threshold Conditions. Our assessment covers expected and stressed credit losses, funding maturity and covenants, and potential redress.
**8F.3 Resilience considerations.** Our wind-down plan explains how existing agreements would continue to be serviced (for example through a sale of the loan book or a back-up servicer) so that customers are not harmed if we stop lending.
ENDOPT: 8F | Consumer credit lenders
OPT: 8G | Firms exercising a lender’s rights and duties | your firm exercises, or has the right to exercise, the lender’s rights and duties under regulated credit agreements.
